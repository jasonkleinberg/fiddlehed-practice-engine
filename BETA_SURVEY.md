# Practice Engine — Beta Tester Survey

*Created 2026-08-14 · Owner: Jason · Audience: PED power-user beta group (Heartbeat) · Beta window: Sept 14 – Oct 2*

---

## What this beta is trying to answer

Two questions, and only two:

1. **Do students actually use it?** — answered by instrumentation, not by asking.
2. **Does it help, and how?** — answered by this survey.

Everything below serves #2. If a question doesn't move one of those two, cut it.

---

## The bias problem (read this before writing any more questions)

FiddleHed students are warm, loyal, and they know Jason built this. "Did it help you?" will come back
yes from nearly everyone, and that yes is worth nothing. The survey has to route around politeness.

Three rules:

- **Ask about behavior, not satisfaction.** What someone *did* last Tuesday is hard to fake politely.
  What someone *thought* about a tool their teacher built is not.
- **Ask for specifics.** Vague questions get vague, generous answers. "Which tune?" forces a real memory.
- **Make it safe to say it didn't land.** Say so explicitly in the intro. Then ask at least one question
  where "I didn't use it" is an easy, expected answer.

## Survey questions

### Section 1 — Did you use it at all?

> This section is deliberately first, and deliberately easy to answer with "no."

1. **When did you last open the Practice Engine?** *(This week / Last week / Longer ago / Never opened it)*
2. If never or barely — **what got in the way?** *(Open text. No wrong answers, this is the useful one.)*

> Anyone answering "Never opened it" should skip to Section 4.

### Section 2 — What did you actually do with it?

3. **Which tune did you use it on most?** *(Open text.)*
4. **What did you actually do?** *(Check all: slowed the tempo down / sped it up / looped a section /
   used the A or B buttons / hid the sheet music / just pressed play and played along / something else)*
5. **Roughly how long was a typical session with it?** *(Under 5 min / 5–15 / 15–30 / 30+)*
6. **Did you come back to it on a different day?** *(Yes, several times / Once or twice / No)*

### Section 3 — The two questions that actually discriminate

7. **What did you use it INSTEAD of?**
   *(A FiddleHed play-along track / a metronome / a YouTube video slowed down / sheet music alone /
   nothing — it was extra practice I wouldn't have done / something else)*

   > **This is the money question.** Substitution tells you what the tool really is. "Nothing — extra
   > practice" is the best possible answer and a completely different result than "your play-along track."

8. **If I removed it tomorrow, how would you feel?**
   *(Very disappointed / Somewhat disappointed / Wouldn't much matter)*

   > Much harder to answer politely than "did you like it." The % answering "very disappointed" is the
   > single cleanest satisfaction number in the whole survey.

9. **Was there a time you thought about using it and didn't?** What happened? *(Open text.)*

   > Friction lives in the negative space. Satisfaction scores never surface this.

### Section 4 — Engine vs. Jason's play-along tracks

> **Framing note — do NOT ask "which is better."** That sets up a competition that doesn't need to happen
> and invites a false binary. Jason's recordings sound like music because a person is playing them; the
> engine does tempo and loops. Those are different practice moments — woodshedding vs. playing along with
> someone. The useful question is *when* each gets reached for.

10. **When you want to practice a tune, which do you reach for first?** *(The Practice Engine / one of
    Jason's play-along tracks / depends — explain below)*
11. **If it depends — what makes you pick one over the other?** *(Open text.)*
12. **Is there anything the play-along tracks give you that the engine doesn't?** *(Open text.)*

### Section 5 — Open

13. **Anything else?** Confusing, broken, missing, annoying. *(Open text.)*

## Success criteria — set these BEFORE the beta opens

Starting proposals. Argue with them; the point is having a number to be wrong about, not having the
right number. A beta with no threshold becomes "well, some people liked it."

| Signal | Source | Proposed bar |
|---|---|---|
| Opened it at all | instrumentation | ≥ 60% of the beta group |
| **Returned on a 2nd separate day** | instrumentation | **≥ 30%** ← the real signal |
| Used tempo OR loop (not just Play) | instrumentation | ≥ 50% of those who opened it |
| "Very disappointed" if removed | Q8 | ≥ 40% |
| "Extra practice I wouldn't have done" | Q7 | ≥ 25% |
| Unanticipated use cases described | Q4, Q9, Q11 | ≥ 3 that Jason didn't predict |

**Read the result as:**

- Hits usage bars + hits Q8 → launch.
- Misses usage, hits Q8 → people like the *idea*; the problem is discovery or friction, not the tool. Fix
  placement before rebuilding anything.
- Hits usage, misses Q8 → it's a novelty. Find out what they wanted it to be.
- Misses both → don't launch it wide yet. Sit with Q2 and Q9.

## Delivery notes

- Keep it under 5 minutes. 13 questions is already near the limit.
- Send it near the **end** of the beta window, not after — memory decays fast and nobody answers a survey
  about last month.
- One reminder, max.
- Pair with 2–3 actual conversations. Open text is good; a person talking is better. The best findings in
  this kind of beta almost never arrive in a form field.
